# CGS Website Information Architecture — Phase 1

Status: LOCKED FOR IMPLEMENTATION
Date: 21 September 2026
Depends on: docs/site-foundation.md, docs/verification-register.md

## 1. Architecture objective

Create one continuous institutional journey in which:

- the homepage orients and routes;
- each child page owns a distinct information problem;
- repeated content is reduced;
- important claims live in one canonical data source;
- every major page ends with a logical next step;
- users can move through the site without being forced into email too early.

The website should behave as a connected system, not six separate pages.

# 4. Homepage architecture

## Primary job

**Orient, establish credibility, and route visitors.**

The homepage must not become the complete About, Programmes, Research and Activity pages compressed together.

## Recommended section order

### H1. Hero — Institutional proposition

Question answered:
**What is CGS?**

Content:
- institutional eyebrow
- one strong proposition
- concise summary
- primary CTA: Explore programmes
- secondary CTA: Explore research
- one meaningful hero image

Keep:
- current editorial hero direction
- strong photography
- study/research routing

Change:
- avoid unsupported programme count
- remove any unverified leadership appointment wording from home

Handoff:
- Programmes
- Research

---

### H2. At-a-glance proof strip

Question answered:
**What kind of Centre is this?**

Purpose:
Provide rapid orientation, not statistics for their own sake.

Recommended proof categories:
- Study pathways
- Research + teaching
- Public engagement

Do not publish an exact programme count until taxonomy is confirmed.

Handoff:
- About / Programmes

---

### H3. Mandate preview

Question answered:
**Why does CGS exist?**

Homepage owns:
- short summary of Research
- Teaching
- Engagement

Homepage does NOT own:
- full explanation of those pillars
- detailed institutional identity
- wider team

CTA:
**About the Centre**

Handoff:
- About

Important change:
Current homepage CTA from mandate goes to the homepage Research section. This weakens the About page. The mandate section should primarily route to About.

---

### H4. Programmes preview

Question answered:
**Can I study here?**

Homepage owns:
- short introduction to the study pathway

- strong learning image

- format

Handoff:

# 6. Programmes architecture

## Primary job

**Help a prospective learner understand the available study pathways and decide what to ask next.**

This page has the strongest decision-making role on the website.

## Recommended section order

### P1. Hero — Study at CGS

Question:
**What can I study here?**

Content:
- broad study proposition
- explicit note that admissions details can change

Primary CTA:
- Browse pathways / jump to programmes

Secondary:
- Contact for current guidance

---

### P2. How the pathways fit together

Question:
**How should I understand these different qualifications?**

This section should explain, without inventing eligibility:
- certificate/diploma-level learning, if confirmed
- postgraduate diploma
- professional/academic masters
- M.Phil./Ph.D.
- Ph.D.

Avoid implying a mandatory linear ladder.

---

### P3. Programme index

Question:
**Which options exist?**

Each programme entry should include only verified information:
- official title
- level/type
- concise purpose
- enquiry action

Do not invent:
- duration
- fees
- application windows
- entry requirements

Until CGS confirms the certificate taxonomy, visibly separate:
- verified SPGS programmes
- other Centre study offerings

---

### P4. Choosing a pathway

Question:
**Which kind of programme might fit my goal?**

Use needs-based orientation, not admissions claims.

Possible decision dimensions:
- professional foundation
- postgraduate transition
- professional advanced study
- academic research
- doctoral research

This is guidance, not eligibility assessment.

---

### P5. Current admissions guidance

Question:
**What should I verify before applying?**

Explain:
- requirements can change
- deadlines can change
- programme arrangements can change

CTA:
Contact CGS

---

### P6. Related exploration

Links:
- Why CGS → About
- Research questions → Research

Then ContactBand.

# 10. Contact architecture

## Primary job

**Route a visitor to the right conversation with minimal friction.**

## Recommended section order

### C1. Hero

Question:
**Can I contact CGS about my kind of enquiry?**

Answer should immediately be yes for:
- programmes
- research
- training
- public/institutional dialogue

---

### C2. Choose your enquiry

Intent cards:
- Study
- Research
- Training / capacity development
- Institutional/public dialogue
- General enquiry

Where a site page can answer the question first:
- Study → Programmes + contact option
- Research → Research + contact option

Do not force email before giving useful context.

---

### C3. Direct contact details

Own:
- verified email
- verified phone numbers
- verified office location

Unverified values stay provisional until Centre sign-off.

---

### C4. What to include in your message

A small practical section can reduce back-and-forth.

For example:
- your name
- programme/topic
- organisation, if relevant
- what you need guidance on

This is editorial guidance and does not require institutional claims.

---

### C5. Information currency note

Explain that:
- deadlines
- admissions requirements
- schedules
can change.

Encourage visitors to confirm current details.

# 14. URL strategy

Keep current primary URLs:

- /
- /about
- /programmes
- /research
- /events
- /activity
- /contact

Do not create new routes during the first redesign pass unless content depth genuinely requires them.

Potential future routes, only when enough verified content exists:

- /programmes/[programme]
- /research/[area]
- /activity/[slug]
- /people/[person]

This avoids premature route complexity.

# 16. Data architecture rule for implementation

Before redesigning the child pages further:

1. Move repeated programme definitions out of the homepage into shared data.
2. Move repeated research-area definitions out of the homepage into shared data.
3. Use one shared source for team/contact/institutional facts.
4. Add internal confidence/status metadata where facts are pending verification if helpful.
5. Page components may reshape shared data for presentation but must not redefine institutional facts locally.

# 17. Phase 1 implementation priorities

## Priority 1 — Navigation and routing logic

Fix the homepage's current tendency to send high-intent visitors directly to mailto/external folders when a dedicated internal page exists.

## Priority 2 — Content ownership

Reduce duplication:
- Home team roster
- full programme detail on Home
- repeated research descriptions

## Priority 3 — Child-page depth

Strengthen:
- Programmes
- Research
- Events
- Activity
- Contact

without bloating About.

## Priority 4 — Shared institutional data

Consolidate repeated facts before visual redesign expands.

---

# 18. Phase 1 acceptance criteria

Phase 1 is complete when:

- every page has one dominant job;
- every homepage section has a defined child-page destination;
- child pages own the detailed version of their subject;
- no major page dead-ends;
- internal links precede premature email actions where appropriate;
- homepage duplication is explicitly identified for removal;
- shared institutional data has a migration plan;
- future route expansion is intentionally deferred;
- the architecture works for students, researchers, partners and general visitors.

After this point, Phase 2 can establish the reusable visual/design system against this architecture.
